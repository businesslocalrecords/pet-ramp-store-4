How to Choose a Dog Car Ramp

A 6-minute read. No upsell — this works whichever ramp you buy.

A dog car ramp is one of the few purchases where a bad measurement shows up immediately: too short for your vehicle, and the "gentle" ramp becomes a wall your dog refuses. Here is how to get it right, in the order that matters.

1. Measure your vehicle first

Measure from the ground to the surface your dog steps onto — the bumper edge or the cargo floor. Do it with a tape measure, in inches. Typical numbers: low sedans around 20–24″, most SUVs 26–30″, lifted trucks 32–42″.

2. Match the ramp length to that height

The ramp's length is the long side of the slope, so the angle it makes is{' '} arcsine(height ÷ length) — longer ramp, gentler slope. The table below is the same math our size finder uses:

Cargo heightCompact (62″ ramp)Extended (70″ ramp)
20″18.8°16.6°
24″22.8°20.1°
28″26.8°23.6°
30″28.9°25.4°
32″31.1°27.2°
36″35.5°30.9°

Lower is gentler. Past roughly 30″ of cargo height, a 62″ ramp starts to feel steep for a senior dog — that is when a 70″+ ramp earns its price.

3. Check the weight rating against your dog

Find the published rating (ours: 150 lb for the Compact, 250 lb for the Extended) and add margin for a growing or heavy dog. A rating quoted with no number at all is a red flag.

4. Look at the walking surface and the rails

Dogs refuse slick decks. Look for a textured, high-traction surface that grips nails, and side rails that stop a nervous dog from slipping off the edge. A ramp your dog will not walk on is a coat rack.

5. Check the feet and stability

Rubberized, non-slip feet at both ends, and a way to lock the ramp down on smooth garage floors (a traction mat does the job). If the feet are bare plastic, expect movement.

6. Check storage before you buy

Note the folded length and weight (ours folds to 31″ / 35″). If it will not fit where you plan to keep it, it will end up in the garage unused.

Red flags on any listing: "lifetime warranty" with no term, "vet approved" with no vet named, thousands of reviews with no dates, and a "gentle slope" quoted in degrees with no height or ramp length to compute it from. Ask for the specifications — a real seller has them.

The short checklist

  • Ramp length matched to your measured cargo height
  • Weight rating comfortably above your dog
  • Textured, high-traction walking surface
  • Side rails along the deck
  • Rubber non-slip feet (plus a way to lock it down indoors)
  • Folded size that fits your storage
